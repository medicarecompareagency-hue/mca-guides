# Topic queue

One article per week, published Monday. The scheduled task takes the top
unpublished topic, writes it, verifies it, and pushes it live.

**To change what comes next, just reorder this file.** The task reads top
to bottom and takes the first line without a date in the Published column.

---

## Why this order

Today is July 2026. Fall Open Enrollment runs **October 15 – December 7**.
A new article typically needs **8–12 weeks** before it ranks for anything
competitive, which means AEP content published in October is too late to
help this season.

So the seasonal topics are front-loaded July through September. They'll be
aged and indexed by the time people start searching in October. The
evergreen high-intent topics fill in after, since those pull traffic
year-round and don't care when they publish.

## Notes on specific entries

**#5 (plan leaving your area)** spikes hard in October when non-renewal
notices land. Worth having live well before.

**#12 (Part B give back)** is heavily searched and heavily abused in
marketing. Write it factually — what it is, which plans have it, the
tradeoffs. No hype. The compliance gate will catch superlatives anyway.

**#13 and #20** contain 2027 figures that **do not exist yet**. CMS
typically publishes them in the fall. If the scheduled task can't verify a
2027 number against a primary source, it will stop and flag rather than
guess. Expect these two to escalate to you if they come up before CMS
publishes — that's the gate working, not a failure.

**#15, 16, 21, 22, 27, 28 (state tables)** follow the format of the Extra
Help article. Indiana is the standout: its MSP limits are dramatically
higher than federal ($2,015 single vs $1,350), which almost nobody writes
about clearly.

**Not in the queue on purpose:** anything comparing named carriers, or
anything that reads as a plan recommendation. That's marketing material
requiring CMS filing, not educational content, and it belongs on a
different track with your compliance contact involved.

## Adding your own

Add a row anywhere. Move it to the top if you want it next. The task takes
the first row with an empty Published column, so position is the only thing
that controls order.

When an article publishes, the task fills in the date. Don't delete
published rows — they're the record of what's already covered, and they
stop the same topic being written twice.
