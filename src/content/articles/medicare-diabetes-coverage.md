Medicare covers most of what people with diabetes need: blood sugar testing supplies, continuous glucose monitors for many people, insulin, screenings, training, and even special shoes. Covered insulin costs no more than $35 for a one-month supply, according to Medicare.gov.

What trips people up is that the coverage is split between Part B and Part D. The same item, like insulin, can be covered under one part or the other depending on how you take it. Knowing which part pays tells you where to look and what you'll owe.

## Testing supplies (Part B)

Part B covers blood sugar meters, test strips, lancets, and lancet devices. CMS sets limits on how many:

- **If you use insulin:** up to 300 test strips and 300 lancets every 3 months.
- **If you don't use insulin:** up to 100 test strips and 100 lancets every 3 months.
- **Either way:** one lancet device every 6 months.

If you need more than that, your doctor has to document why it's medically necessary. Without that paperwork, extra strips can be denied.

For supplies and equipment under Part B, you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after you meet your Part B deductible (CMS, cms.gov).

## Continuous glucose monitors (Part B)

Part B covers continuous glucose monitors (CGMs), including the sensors and transmitters, for people with diabetes who meet the rules. According to CMS, you need a CGM prescribed for its approved use, and you have to either be treated with insulin or have a history of problematic low blood sugar. You also need a visit with your doctor within 6 months before the CGM is ordered.

That second option is worth knowing. You don't have to be on insulin to qualify if you've had trouble with dangerous lows.

## Insulin: Part B or Part D, depending on how you take it

This is where most of the confusion lives.

**If you use a traditional insulin pump** that counts as durable medical equipment, Part B covers the pump and the insulin used in it. You pay 20% of the approved amount for the pump after the Part B deductible, and the insulin is capped at $35 a month (CMS, cms.gov).

**If you inject insulin with pens or syringes,** Part B doesn't cover it. Your Medicare drug plan (Part D) or Medicare Advantage plan with drug coverage does.

**If you use a disposable "patch" pump,** Part B doesn't cover it either. A drug plan may. Medicare.gov warns that patch pumps may cost more than $35 because they fall outside the $35 limit.

Under all three ways of getting covered insulin, Medicare.gov says you pay no more than $35 for a one-month supply and no deductible for the insulin itself. If you get Extra Help, you pay less than $35 (Medicare.gov).

One gap people hit: Part B doesn't cover insulin syringes, needles, or other insulin supplies. Part D plans may cover them.

## Screenings and prevention

**Diabetes screenings.** If you're at risk for diabetes, Medicare covers up to 2 screenings a year. You pay $0 if your provider accepts assignment (Medicare.gov).

**Medicare Diabetes Prevention Program.** For people with prediabetes, this program offers 16 weekly group sessions over 6 months, then 6 monthly follow-up sessions. You must have prediabetes test results, a body mass index of 25 or more (23 or more if you're Asian), and no history of type 1 or type 2 diabetes or end-stage renal disease. You pay $0 if you qualify (Medicare.gov).

## Training and nutrition

**Diabetes self-management training.** Medicare covers up to 10 hours of initial training, 1 hour one-on-one and 9 hours in a group, within 12 months, plus 2 hours of follow-up each year after. You need a written order. You pay 20% of the approved amount after the Part B deductible (Medicare.gov).

**Medical nutrition therapy.** If you have diabetes or kidney disease and a doctor's referral, Medicare covers sessions with a registered dietitian or qualified nutrition professional. You pay $0 if you qualify (Medicare.gov).

## Feet and eyes

**Foot exams.** If you have diabetes-related nerve damage in your lower legs that raises your risk of losing a limb, Medicare covers a foot exam every 6 months by a podiatrist or foot care specialist. You pay 20% after the Part B deductible (Medicare.gov).

**Therapeutic shoes.** If you have a qualifying foot condition, Medicare covers one pair of depth shoes and 3 pairs of inserts, or one pair of custom-molded shoes and 2 extra pairs of inserts, each calendar year. You need a prescription and a fitting by a qualified provider (CMS).

**Glaucoma tests.** Diabetes puts you in the high-risk group, so Medicare covers a glaucoma test once every 12 months, done or supervised by a licensed eye doctor. You pay 20% after the Part B deductible (Medicare.gov).

## What Medicare doesn't cover

Medicare.gov lists several things that aren't covered for people with diabetes, including eyeglasses and exams for glasses (except after cataract surgery), orthopedic shoes, cosmetic surgery, and routine physical exams. CMS also lists weight loss programs among items not covered.

## Common mistakes

**Running out of strips.** If you test more often than the limits allow, ask your doctor to document why before you order. That paperwork is what lets Medicare pay for the extra.

**Assuming your pump insulin and your pen insulin are covered the same way.** They're not. One goes through Part B, the other through your drug plan. Check that your drug plan covers the specific insulin you use.

**Forgetting the training benefit.** Self-management training is one of the most underused benefits. Ask your doctor for the written order.

**Not checking Medicare Advantage rules.** Medicare Advantage plans cover these same items but may require specific suppliers or prior approval. Call the plan before you order supplies.

## Where to get help

For questions about your coverage, call 1-800-MEDICARE or visit Medicare.gov. Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers free one-on-one counseling.
