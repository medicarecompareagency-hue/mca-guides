In Tennessee, a single person with Medicare can get help paying their Part B premium if their monthly income is $1,816 or less and their savings are $9,950 or less. For a married couple, the limits are $2,455 a month and $14,910 in savings. Those are the 2026 federal limits, and Tennessee uses them (Medicare.gov; TennCare). Lower incomes qualify for more help, up to having your deductibles and copays paid too.

These programs are called Medicare Savings Programs. In Tennessee, TennCare runs them, and you may see them called "Medicare cost sharing" programs on TennCare's website. If you qualify for one, you also get Extra Help with your prescription drugs automatically.

## The three main programs and their limits

Tennessee has three Medicare Savings Programs most people would apply for. Each one has a monthly income limit and a resource (savings) limit. These are the 2026 figures from Medicare.gov and Social Security's program manual (ssa.gov). The income limits include the standard $20 a month that isn't counted.

| Program | What TennCare pays | Income limit, single | Income limit, couple |
|---|---|---|---|
| QMB | Part A and Part B premiums, plus deductibles, coinsurance, and copays | $1,350 a month | $1,824 a month |
| SLMB | Part B premium only | $1,616 a month | $2,184 a month |
| QI-1 | Part B premium only | $1,816 a month | $2,455 a month |

The resource limit is the same for all three: $9,950 for one person and $14,910 for a married couple (Medicare.gov; TennCare Eligibility Reference Guide).

**QMB (Qualified Medicare Beneficiary)** is the fullest help. TennCare says it pays Medicare premiums, deductibles, and coinsurance. Medicare.gov adds that if you have QMB, Medicare providers aren't allowed to bill you for Medicare-covered deductibles, coinsurance, and copays. You may still owe a small Medicaid copayment.

**SLMB (Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary)** pays your Part B premium only.

**QI-1 (Qualifying Individual)** also pays your Part B premium only. TennCare says you can't be enrolled in another TennCare Medicaid category at the same time, and it pays QI-1 premiums as long as federal funds are available.

There's a fourth program, **QDWI (Qualified Disabled and Working Individual)**, which pays the Part A premium for certain people under 65 with disabilities who went back to work and lost premium-free Part A. Its 2026 limits are $5,405 a month and $4,000 in resources for one person (ssa.gov).

## What that help is worth

The standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month in 2026 and goes to $209.40 a month in 2027 (Medicare.gov). If that premium comes out of your Social Security check now, any of the three programs above means it stops coming out. That money goes back into your monthly check.

With QMB, you also stop owing the Part B deductible, which is $283 in 2026 and $292 in 2027 (Medicare.gov), and the 20% coinsurance you'd normally pay on most Part B services.

## Extra Help with prescription drugs

Extra Help, also called the Low-Income Subsidy, is a federal program run by Social Security. It lowers what you pay for Medicare drug coverage. The limits are the same in every state, Tennessee included.

For 2026, Medicare.gov lists the Extra Help limits as $23,940 a year in income and $18,090 in resources for one person, or $32,460 in income and $36,100 in resources for a married couple.

For 2027, Medicare.gov says people with Extra Help pay:

- $0 for the plan premium and $0 for the deductible
- Up to $5.80 for each generic drug and up to $14.40 for each brand-name drug
- $0 per covered drug once total drug costs reach $2,400 for the year

People with full Medicaid or QMB pay no more than $4.90 per covered drug in 2027 (Medicare.gov).

You get Extra Help automatically, without a separate application, if you have full Medicaid, a Medicare Savings Program that pays your Part B premium, or SSI (Medicare.gov). Everyone else applies through Social Security.

## What people in Tennessee get wrong

**Looking at an old chart.** TennCare's website has more than one page listing these programs, and they don't all show the same numbers. Some show older, lower limits. TennCare's Eligibility Reference Guide lists resource limits of $9,950 and $14,910, which match the 2026 federal figures. If you're comparing your income to a chart and the numbers don't match what's above, check the date on the chart.

**Assuming the federal limits are the end of the story.** Medicare.gov notes that you may still qualify even if your income or resources are a little over the federal limits, because some states don't count certain types or amounts of income or resources. If you're close, apply and let TennCare decide.

**Mixing up TennCare and a Medicare Savings Program.** Getting QMB, SLMB, or QI-1 doesn't mean you have full TennCare Medicaid. TennCare describes these as cost-sharing help, not full Medicaid benefits. Your Medicare stays your main coverage.

**Not applying for Extra Help because you got turned down for Medicaid.** The Extra Help limits are much higher than the Medicare Savings Program limits. A single person over $1,816 a month in income can still fall under the Extra Help income limit. If one says no, apply for the other.

**Forgetting the limits change.** Income limits are tied to the federal poverty guidelines and are updated every year. The figures above are for 2026. Check the current numbers before you apply.

## How to apply in Tennessee

For a Medicare Savings Program, TennCare lists these ways to apply:

- Online through TennCare Connect
- By phone with TennCare Connect at 855-259-0701
- By mail to TennCare Connect, P.O. Box 305240, Nashville, TN 37230-5240, or by fax at 1-855-315-0669
- In person at your local Department of Human Services office

TennCare also lists 1-877-801-0044 for help applying for a Medicare Savings Program.

For Extra Help, apply online at ssa.gov or call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778). Medicare.gov notes you can apply for Extra Help and a Medicare Savings Program at the same time.

For free, unbiased one-on-one help, you can contact your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) through shiphelp.org, or call 1-800-MEDICARE.
