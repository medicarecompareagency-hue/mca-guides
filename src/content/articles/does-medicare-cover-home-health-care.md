Yes, but probably not in the way most families hope. Medicare covers part-time, skilled care at home, such as nursing visits and physical therapy, for people who are homebound and whose doctor orders it. It does not cover someone to stay with you around the clock, and it doesn't pay for help with bathing, dressing, or housework when that's the only help you need.

If you qualify, though, the coverage is good. For covered home health services, you pay $0, according to Medicare.gov.

## Who qualifies

Medicare.gov lists several conditions. All of them have to be true.

**You need skilled care, part-time or intermittent.** That means care that has to come from a nurse or therapist, given in visits rather than all day.

**You're homebound.** This is the rule that confuses people the most. Medicare.gov says you're homebound if both of these apply:

- Leaving home isn't recommended because of your condition, or you have trouble leaving without help, such as a cane, wheelchair, walker, or another person, because of an illness or injury.
- You're normally unable to leave home, and leaving takes a lot of effort.

Homebound doesn't mean you can never go out. Medicare.gov says you can still qualify if you leave home for medical treatment, for short and infrequent non-medical trips such as religious services, or to attend adult day care.

**A doctor or other allowed provider orders it.** A doctor, or another allowed provider such as a nurse practitioner, must see you in person, confirm that you need home health care, and order it.

**A Medicare-certified agency provides it.** The home health agency has to be certified by Medicare. Not every agency that advertises home care is.

## What Medicare covers

According to Medicare.gov, covered home health services include:

- Part-time or intermittent skilled nursing care
- Physical therapy, occupational therapy, and speech-language therapy
- Medical social services
- Part-time or intermittent home health aide services, but only when you're also getting skilled nursing or therapy
- Injectable osteoporosis drugs for women who meet certain conditions
- Durable medical equipment and medical supplies for use at home

One detail on occupational therapy: Medicare.gov says it has to start alongside skilled nursing, physical therapy, or speech therapy. After that, you can continue occupational therapy on its own.

## What "part-time" actually means

Medicare.gov defines part-time or intermittent care as generally up to 8 hours a day of combined skilled nursing and home health aide care, with a maximum of 28 hours a week. For a short time, your provider can approve more, up to 35 hours a week, if it's needed.

There's no cap on the number of visits. Medicare.gov says eligible people can get unlimited home health visits as long as they continue to qualify.

## What Medicare doesn't cover

This list is where expectations and reality usually split. Medicare.gov says home health coverage does not include:

- 24-hour-a-day care at home
- Meals delivered to your home
- Homemaker services, like shopping, cleaning, and laundry, when they aren't related to your care plan
- Custodial or personal care, like bathing, dressing, or using the bathroom, when that's the only care you need

That last one matters. If Mom needs help getting dressed and bathing but doesn't need a nurse or therapist, Medicare home health won't pay for an aide. The aide is covered only as part of a skilled care plan.

## What you pay

For covered home health services, you pay $0 (Medicare.gov).

Medical equipment is different. For Medicare-covered equipment like a walker or hospital bed, you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after you meet your Part B deductible (Medicare.gov).

Before care starts, the agency should tell you how much Medicare will pay. If the agency plans to give you something Medicare doesn't cover, it should give you a written notice called an Advance Beneficiary Notice (ABN) first. Read it before you sign. It's how you find out you'd be paying.

## If you have a Medicare Advantage plan

Medicare Advantage plans have to cover the same home health services Original Medicare covers, but the plan sets its own rules for which agencies you can use and whether you need approval first. Call the plan before care begins and ask which home health agencies are in its network.

## Common mistakes

**Thinking you need a hospital stay first.** Medicare.gov's eligibility rules for home health don't include a prior hospital stay. What you need is a provider's order, a homebound status, and a need for skilled care.

**Assuming "homebound" means bedbound.** It doesn't. Going to the doctor, to church now and then, or to adult day care doesn't disqualify you.

**Expecting long-term caregiving.** Home health is designed for skilled care in visits. Ongoing personal care, by itself, falls outside it. Medicaid or long-term care insurance may help with that kind of care, depending on your situation.

**Using an agency that isn't Medicare-certified.** Ask directly. If the agency isn't certified, Medicare won't pay.

## Where to get help

You can search for Medicare-certified home health agencies on Medicare.gov's Care Compare tool, or call 1-800-MEDICARE. Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) gives free counseling if you have questions about your coverage.
