# What Is a Medicare Special Needs Plan (SNP)?

A Medicare Special Needs Plan, or SNP, is a type of Medicare Advantage plan that only certain people can join. Medicare describes it as a plan that provides benefits and services to people with specific severe and chronic diseases, certain health care needs, or who also have Medicaid. If you don't fit one of those groups, you can't enroll.

An SNP isn't a better or worse version of a regular Medicare Advantage plan. It's the same kind of plan, built around one group of people and open only to them.

## What every SNP has in common

According to Medicare.gov, SNPs are either HMO or PPO plan types, and they cover the same Part A and Part B benefits that all Medicare Advantage plans cover. To join one, you need:

- Medicare Part A and Part B
- A home address inside the plan's service area
- Eligibility for the specific type of SNP

Every SNP must include Medicare drug coverage (Part D). SNPs also include care coordination services, and they tailor their benefits, provider choices, and drug lists to the group they serve. Medicare.gov says whether you can keep your current doctors varies by plan, so check the network and drug list against *your* doctors and *your* prescriptions.

## The three types

| Type | Who it's for |
|---|---|
| **Dual Eligible SNP (D-SNP)** | People who have both Medicare and Medicaid. CMS describes members as entitled to Medicare and to medical assistance from a state Medicaid plan, including full Medicaid and the Medicare Savings Programs (QMB, SLMB, QI, QDWI). States vary in how they define these categories. |
| **Chronic Condition SNP (C-SNP)** | People with a severe or disabling chronic condition. A plan can limit membership to one condition or a group of related ones. CMS has approved 15 qualifying conditions, including diabetes, chronic heart failure, dementia, cancer (excluding pre-cancer or in-situ status), chronic lung disorders, stroke, and end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis. A plan must confirm you have a qualifying condition. |
| **Institutional SNP (I-SNP)** | People who, for 90 days or longer, have had or are expected to need the level of care provided in a long-term care facility. I-SNPs can also enroll people at home who need that level of care, but only if an independent third party with the right credentials assesses them using the state's standard evaluation tool. |

## When you can join or switch

SNPs follow enrollment rules that differ from regular Medicare Advantage. Medicare.gov lists them under Special Enrollment Periods:

- **Chronic condition:** If you have a qualifying chronic condition, you can join a C-SNP at any time. It's a one-time chance. Once you join, this special enrollment period is used up, though regular enrollment periods still apply.
- **Medicaid or Extra Help:** You can make changes to your coverage once a calendar month, and changes take effect the first day of the following month. This doesn't apply if you've been identified as at-risk or potentially at-risk under a Part D drug management program.
- **Living in an institution:** If you live in a nursing home or similar facility, you can join, switch, or drop coverage as long as you live there and for two full months after the month you move out.

Since January 1, 2025, certain people with full Medicaid benefits can also enroll once a month in a highly or fully integrated dual eligible plan, but only when enrolling in the same company's Medicaid managed care plan. CMS spells out the details in its implementation guidance.

## What people get wrong

- **Thinking an SNP is a separate kind of Medicare.** It's a Medicare Advantage plan. You still need both Part A and Part B.
- **Assuming costs are the same everywhere.** Medicare.gov says costs vary by plan, and some plans charge a premium in addition to the Part B premium. For people with Medicaid, Medicare.gov says most costs will be covered, but your state Medicaid office can tell you exactly what that means.
- **Assuming your doctors are in the network.** Some SNPs require in-network providers except in emergencies. Others offer out-of-network coverage, usually at a higher cost. Check each doctor, specialist, and pharmacy before you enroll.
- **Forgetting that eligibility can end.** You can stay in an SNP only if you continue to meet the condition the plan serves. If you lose Medicaid or your situation changes, you may qualify for a special enrollment period to pick new coverage. Keep your plan's notices and respond to them.

## How to check what's available where you live

The Medicare Plan Finder at medicare.gov/plan-compare lets you look at plan types and costs for your ZIP code. SNP availability depends on your county. Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers free counseling, and your state Medicaid office can answer questions about the Medicaid side of a D-SNP.
