# What Is the Medicare Part B Give Back Benefit?

The Part B give back benefit is a feature of some Medicare Advantage plans. The plan applies money toward your Part B premium, so less of that premium comes out of you. Medicare's official booklet puts it this way: some plans may have a $0 premium and "may help pay all or part of your Part B premium." Not every plan does this, and the amount differs from plan to plan.

"Give back" is a marketing nickname. Medicare's own term is a Part B premium reduction. If you're hunting for it in official materials, that's the phrase to look for.

## How it works

Everyone with Part B pays a monthly premium. For 2026, CMS set the standard amount at $202.90, and Medicare.gov says it can be higher depending on your income. The premium goes up or down from year to year.

A Medicare Advantage plan can choose to use part of the money it receives from Medicare to lower that premium for its members. CMS describes it as an amount the plan applies "to reduce the Part B premium for enrollees under the contract for the contract year." The Social Security Administration's handbook says Social Security takes the reduction into account once CMS notifies it. For most people the result shows up as a smaller Part B premium deduction from their Social Security check, instead of a separate payment to them.

## What it does not change

**You still have Part B, and you still pay for it.** Medicare.gov says you must have Part B and keep paying your Part B premium to stay in a Medicare Advantage plan. A plan with this benefit lowers what you pay. It doesn't remove the requirement.

**You may still pay a plan premium.** Medicare.gov says many plans charge a premium in addition to the Part B premium. A plan can offer a Part B reduction and also have its own monthly premium, so look at both numbers.

**It doesn't change your copays, deductibles, or network.** The benefit lowers one bill. Doctor and hospital costs, drug costs, and which providers you can see are set separately by each plan.

## What people get wrong

**Thinking it's a check in the mail.** For most people it works through the Part B premium itself, so you'd see it as a smaller deduction. Where the reduction shows up depends on how you pay your Part B premium, so ask the plan how it will work for you.

**Thinking the amount stays the same.** Medicare.gov says Medicare Advantage costs and rules "can change each year." A plan that reduces your Part B premium this year may reduce it by a different amount next year, or drop the benefit. Each fall, the plan's Annual Notice of Change is where any change should be listed. Reading it matters more than assuming.

**Treating it as a sure thing.** CMS says plans pay for the reduction from rebates they choose to allocate to it in their yearly bid. Those are decisions the plan makes each year, which is one more reason the amount can move. A plan with the benefit isn't automatically a better fit than one without it. What matters is your total cost across premiums, copays, drugs, and your doctors.

**Ignoring the rest of the plan.** A lower Part B premium means little if your doctors are out of network or your prescriptions cost more. Check the provider directory and drug list the same way you would for any plan.

**Assuming everyone benefits the same way.** The reduction applies to the Part B premium you owe. If you have help from your state paying your Part B premium, such as through a Medicare Savings Program, ask the plan and your state Medicaid office how the two fit together before counting on any savings.

## Why the amount can differ

Because each plan decides how to use its rebates, two plans in the same county can treat the Part B premium differently. One may reduce it by a set amount, another may not reduce it at all. Plans also set their rules for the year ahead, so the reduction you see for next year is the one in that plan's current-year materials. Don't carry last year's number forward.

## Questions to ask

- Does this plan reduce the Part B premium, and by how much for the current year?
- How will it show up: in my Social Security deduction, or another way?
- Is there also a monthly plan premium?
- Did the amount change from last year's Annual Notice of Change?
- Are my doctors and prescriptions covered?

Plan details are listed at Medicare.gov/plan-compare or at 1-800-MEDICARE. Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers free counseling if you want help reading the numbers.
